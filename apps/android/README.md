# The Android shell, and Play's setup checklist

The app on Play is a **TWA** — a Trusted Web Activity, which is real Chrome
running `apps/web`. There is no Android source to speak of; the shell is
generated by Bubblewrap from `twa-manifest.json`. A change to `apps/web` reaches
Android and iOS both. Nothing in `apps/ios/` reaches this.

Build, signing and the assetlinks traps are in `BACKLOG.md` server lane 10. What
follows is the other half: **Play's "set up your app" tasks**, which are on no
other list and which are the difference between an internal testing build and
anything a member can install.

Everything below is meant to be copied into the console rather than decided
again. Where a decision is still open it says so, in the section it belongs to.

## 2. App content

### Privacy policy

```
https://www.loveplusone.app/privacy
```

Already serving. `/terms` is beside it.

### Ads

**No.** There is no advertising SDK in the app. `PLAY_NO_ADVERTISING` in
`packages/config/src/play-data-safety.ts` holds the reasoning, and it is the
same fact that makes Apple's tracking answer false — a test ties the two
together so one cannot change without the other.

### App access — **the hard one, and it is not finished**

A reviewer has to get in, and getting in means a phone OTP they cannot receive.
The liveness check is not the problem it looks like: a reviewer account that
Kevin has already taken through onboarding is past that gate permanently, so the
only live barrier is the sign-in code.

What to tell Play, once the account exists:

```
All functionality requires a verified account. Sign in with the phone
number below; the app will ask for a 6-digit code, which is fixed for
this test number and does not require receiving an SMS.

  Phone: <test number>
  Code:  <fixed OTP>

This account has already completed identity verification, so no camera
or selfie is needed. Creating a NEW account does require a one-time
identity check, which is why the test account is provided.
```

**Hosted Supabase supports this, and the path is VERIFIED end to end** —
2026-08-27, against the live project rather than reasoned about. Authentication →
Sign In / Providers → Phone → **"Test phone numbers and OTPs"**, a
comma-separated list of `<phone number>=<otp>` pairs, with a date field beside it
after which they stop working.

Proof, straight against the auth API: requesting a code for the test number
answers `{"message_id":"test-otp"}` — Supabase saying outright that it sent no
SMS — and verifying the fixed code returns a session with
`phone_confirmed_at` set. That is the reviewer's whole journey, minus the
screens.

Three things learned doing it:

- **Name the button in the instructions.** The account must be signed IN to, not
  created — "Get started" and "Sign in" are different doors and only one of them
  works once the account exists. This exact confusion cost a round trip here,
  and a reviewer who takes the wrong one sees an error and files a rejection
  rather than asking.
- **Set the expiry past any plausible review.** A first submission of a dating
  app that handles health data is not a same-week review. If the pair expires
  mid-review the reviewer is locked out, and that returns as a rejection rather
  than a question.
- **Use a reserved number.** `555-0100`–`555-0199` cannot belong to anybody,
  which is why Supabase's own example uses one. A real number here is a
  guessable door into an account with real rows in it — and worse, taking it
  back OUT of the list makes it a live number again, at which point a sign-in
  attempt sends a real SMS to whoever owns it.

An earlier version of this section claimed the field needs the country code and
that a ten-digit entry matches nothing. **That was wrong** — it was inferred from
a probe that caught the setting mid-edit. Supabase matches the app's `+1…` E.164
form against what is stored without help. Left recorded because the reasoning
looked sound and was not.

### Content rating

The questionnaire is answered per-category. This app is a **dating / social**
app with user-generated content and direct messaging. The answers that decide
the rating:

- **Users can interact** — yes. Text, voice notes and photos, one-to-one and in
  rooms.
- **Users can share content** — yes, including photos.
- **Users' location is shared with other users** — approximate only. Distance is
  derived from a value rounded to ~1.1km before storage; an exact position is
  never held.
- **Sexual content / references** — this is a dating app whose subject is sexual
  health. Answer honestly rather than minimising: it references sexual health and
  sexual orientation throughout. It contains no pornography and no explicit
  imagery, and photo moderation exists.
- **Violence, gambling, drugs, profanity** — none.

Expect **Mature 17+ / PEGI 18** and do not fight it. A dating app for people with
HIV and HSV that rated itself as suitable for children would be the wrong kind of
wrong, and a rating obtained by understating gets pulled later.

### Target audience and content

**18 and over only.** Do not tick any bracket below it. The app is 18+ in its own
terms and the whole product assumes adults. Ticking a younger bracket pulls in
Families policy obligations that are neither wanted nor honestly answerable.

### Health apps declaration

This app collects **health information** — see the Data safety mapping below,
which declares `Health and fitness → Health info`. Play asks a separate set of
health questions for apps that do. It is **not** a medical device, offers no
diagnosis or treatment, and gives no clinical advice; U=U is stated as a fact
about HIV treatment with a self-reported badge, which is health _information_
rather than health _guidance_. Answer accordingly, and do not claim any clinical
function the app does not have.

### Data safety

**Do not answer this from memory.** Every answer is worked out and held by a test
in `packages/config/src/play-data-safety.ts`:

- `PLAY_DATA_SAFETY` — each data type, whether it is collected, shared,
  processed ephemerally, optional, and its purposes, with the reason in words.
- `PLAY_NOT_COLLECTED` — the NO answers, which are claims rather than blanks.
- `PLAY_SECURITY` — encrypted in transit, deletion, and the deletion URL.

`play-data-safety.test.ts` fails when these stop agreeing with the Apple labels,
which are themselves held against the migrations. So the chain runs from a new
column all the way to both stores, and the two forms cannot drift apart.

Two things in there worth knowing before you open the form:

- **The liveness selfie is declared on Play and not on Apple**, and that is
  correct rather than inconsistent. Play has a "processed ephemerally" answer —
  collected, used, never retained — which is exactly what the check does. Apple's
  form has no such option, which is why that one sits in `NOT_COLLECTED` with a
  note held for counsel.
- **Play needs a deletion URL that works without the app installed**, separately
  from the in-app path. That is `PLAY_SECURITY.deletionUrl` and it points at the
  policy's deletion section. The in-app half is Settings, which `requestDeletion`
  implements and `legal.ts` promises in as many words.
